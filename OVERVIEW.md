# Context

*More than a Hack 2027* is organised by **Metrica Sports**, **FC Barcelona** — through the Barça
Innovation Hub — and the **Mobile World Congress**, and takes place at the Talent Arena in
Barcelona. It brings together football analysts, data scientists and engineers to work on a real
problem in professional football, using authentic match data from FC Barcelona.

Participants receive a full professional match: two video recordings, automatic tracking data with
the ball for both, automatic tactical tagging, and camera-to-pitch homographies. The task is to
determine, for every frame of the match, which team is in possession of the ball.

Far from a theoretical exercise, this is a problem Metrica works on in production. It reflects
Metrica Sports' commitment to democratising access to elite analytics and FC Barcelona's ambition
to lead in applied sports science and technology.

# Objective

**The 2027 edition changes format.** The 2026 hackathon was open-ended: teams chose their own
questions, explored freely, and a jury assessed the results. That format rewarded breadth and
creativity, and it made comparing entries a matter of judgement.

2027 asks one question, and answers it with a number.

Every team solves the same problem on the same data and is scored by the same script against the
same hidden ground truth. The ranking is automatic and reproducible: there is no jury, no
presentation to argue from, and no ambiguity about who did better. What teams compete on is the
quality of the method, and nothing else.

The problem was chosen because it is genuinely unsolved and genuinely useful. Possession is the
first thing any analyst wants segmented and the first thing any automatic system gets wrong at the
margins — around restarts, contested balls and deflections. A system that does this well on
automatic, uncorrected tracking would be used.

# The challenge

For each of the **199,319 frames** of the tactical video, output one label:

| label | meaning |
|---|---|
| `H` | home team (Barcelona) in possession, ball alive |
| `A` | away team (Athletic Club) in possession, ball alive |
| `D` | dead ball — not in play |
| `X` | no answer — the system declines to guess |

A submission is a CSV with 199,319 rows. That is the entire deliverable that is scored.

**What is deliberately not asked.** Teams are not asked who "owns" a dead ball — that is a
different and far more ambiguous problem, and when the ball is dead the answer is simply `D`.

**What is deliberately out of scope.** This is not a multi-object tracking challenge and not
game-state reconstruction. Tracking, tagging and homographies are provided so that no team has to
rebuild detection, re-identification and camera calibration before reaching the actual question.
Teams that want to improve or replace them may do so; it is a starting point, not a constraint.

## Automatic Tracking Data (ATD)

Generated by Metrica's computer-vision algorithms. It provides positional and velocity information
**at team level** — that is, it does **not** include player identifiers — together with the
position of the ball. There are 46 track slots for 22 players; slots appear, vanish and swap
identity as the tracker loses and re-acquires people.

It is automatic and uncorrected. It has gaps, fragmented tracks and identity switches, and **the
ball is absent on 41% of graded frames**. Handling that noise is part of the problem.

## Smart Tagging

Metrica Sports' Smart Tagging provides **automatic** identification and annotation of tactical game
phases and set-piece plays. 638 instances in this match.

- **Build Up.** The set of offensive actions whereby a team initiates and develops possession from
  its own half, aiming to overcome the opponent's first line of pressure and organise the advance
  toward more forward zones. *Tags: Half, Team.*
- **Progression.** Offensive actions advancing the ball primarily through the middle third,
  overcoming rival lines via passes, carries or support play. *Tags: Half, Team.*
- **Creating Chances.** Offensive actions in the final third that generate clear danger or
  finishing situations — key passes, assists, actions leaving a teammate in an advantageous
  position. *Tags: Half, Team.*
- **Defending in Attacking / Middle / Defensive Third.** Defensive actions performed in each third
  of the pitch: pressure and space control high up, protection of central spaces to limit
  progression, and marking, covering and blocking near the team's own goal. *Tags: Half, Team.*
- **Attacking Transition.** The counter-attack: actions immediately after recovering the ball,
  exploiting the rival's defensive disorder. *Tags: Half, Team.*
- **Defensive Transition.** Actions immediately after losing possession — reorganising, protecting
  key spaces, pressing or dropping back. *Tags: Half, Team.*
- **Set Pieces.** Situations initiated after a stoppage: corners, free kicks, throw-ins, goal
  kicks, kick-offs. *Tags: Half, Team, Type.*
- **Goals.** The ball completely crossing the goal line into the rival net. *Tags: Half, Team.*
- **Long Ball.** A direct long-distance pass, generally from the defensive third, to gain territory
  quickly or attack the space behind the defence. *Tags: Half, Team.*
- **Ball in Final Third.** The team maintains or plays the ball in the zone closest to the rival
  goal. *Tags: Half, Team, Side, Max players in the box.*
- **Ball in the Box.** The ball enters or is played inside the rival box, by pass, cross or carry.
  *Tags: Half, Team, Direction of ball entry, Max players in the box.*
- **Players in the Box.** The quantity of attacking players inside the rival box during an
  offensive action. *Tags: Half, Team, Max players in the box.*

**Tags:** *Team* — the team performing the event. *Half* — first or second. *Type* — the set-piece
category. *Max players in the box* — the maximum number of attacking players in the box at the
moment of the event. *Side* — left, centre or right. *Direction of ball entry* — diagonal, vertical
or horizontal.

**Important.** Smart Tagging is not manually ground-truthed. It is inferred through automated
processes and may contain both false positives and false negatives. It is a strong free prior, not
an answer key.

## Homography

One 3×3 matrix per video frame, mapping normalised pitch coordinates to image pixels — the same
0–1 convention the tracking uses. Provided as CSV, with an explicit `valid` flag: the tactical
recording is usable on every frame, the broadcast on 65.8%.

## Use of Large Language Models

Participants may use LLMs anywhere in their pipeline — commercial (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini) or
open-source, hosted or local. There is no restriction on model choice.

Note the difference from 2026: the use of LLMs is **not itself an evaluation criterion** this year.
Nothing is scored except the possession file. An LLM is worth using if it makes the answer better.

# How the task is scored

Ranked on **`spellF1`**; **`macroF1`** is reported beside it and breaks ties.

- **`macroF1`** — the mean per-class F1 over `H`, `A` and `D`, frame by frame. The per-frame view.
- **`spellF1`** — possessions matched one-to-one against the truth by temporal overlap, averaged
  over five overlap thresholds. Each possession counts once, as a coach counts it.

The ranking uses `spellF1` because **no per-frame metric can represent possession**: the
overwhelming majority of graded frames sit in the middle of a possession where nothing is
happening, and the longest possession of a match counts hundreds of times the shortest. A system
can score near 0.97 per frame while reporting far more possessions than really happened.

The repository demonstrates this with its own baselines — the same algorithm four times, differing
only in a smoothing window:

| baseline | macroF1 | spellF1 |
|---|---|---|
| `baseline_raw` | 0.6532 | 0.0486 |
| `baseline_smooth25` | **0.6752** | 0.1996 |
| `baseline_smooth50` | 0.6724 | **0.2433** |
| `baseline_smooth125` | 0.6356 | 0.2242 |

`macroF1` moves by 0.04 across the sweep and prefers one variant; `spellF1` moves by a factor of
five and prefers another. **`baseline_smooth50`, at spellF1 0.2433, is the published floor.**

Teams receive the ground truth for the first **5 minutes** of play to verify their pipeline. The
ranking is computed against the full match, which stays private until the competition closes. No
statistic of that ground truth — its possession count, mean duration or possession share — is
published, since those are precisely the quantities a submission could be tuned against rather
than earned. Full detail: [EVALUATION.md](EVALUATION.md).

# Evaluation criteria

**The score decides the ranking.** `spellF1` on the private full-match ground truth, ties broken by
`macroF1`. There is no jury component and no subjective assessment of the approach.

The repository and the technical document are not scored — they are what make the score mean
something. A submission whose result cannot be reproduced from its repository is not a result, and
a submission not generated by an algorithm is not a submission.

**<TBD: whether a reproducibility check gates prizes.>** Recommended: organisers re-run the winning
team's repository against the provided data and confirm it reproduces the submitted file before
results are final.

This is the sharpest difference from 2026, where originality, technical solidity, use of LLMs,
football relevance and presentation quality were all weighed together. In 2027 those qualities
matter only insofar as they produce a better number.

# Rules

The full rules are in [RULES.md](RULES.md). In summary:

**Permitted — any method**, provided (1) the organisers can access and inspect it, and (2) the
technical document explains it. Any language, framework, model or external data. Improving or
replacing the provided tracking with your own is explicitly welcome.

**Not permitted:**

- **Annotating the data by hand.** The labels must be produced by an algorithm, whether or not any
  answer key was involved. Hand-labelled data used to train or validate a model is fine; a
  hand-labelled submission is not.
- **Using the private ground truth**, or anything derived from it, beyond the published 5 minutes.
- **Submitting a file the team's repository cannot regenerate.**
